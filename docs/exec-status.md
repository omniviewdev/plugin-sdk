# Exec v2: hand-over status

[Docs home](README.md) · [Exec: run commands in a target](exec.md) · [Before any tag that ships `exec.v2`](exec-before-any-tag.md)

This page hands the exec v2 lane to the next engineer. It says what is built, what remains, where the work lives and how to prove it, so that nothing needs to be asked of the people who built it.

**State at `b9cedd6fc5aecbaf4fe6aab8f4a329f026dbff66` (`feature/exec-v2` after PR #92).** The contract, the engine, `Open`, `Run` and `RunStream`, the control frames, sessions that outlive their stream, the keepalive and the fixes found by a real Kubernetes provider are built, reviewed and merged into `feature/exec-v2`. Nothing is tagged, and **the tag gate is not cleared**: it blocks on C3 (process), B1.4, B1.5, B1.6, B1.7, B1.10, B2 and T1, is conditional on B1.3, and waits for two decisions (S2 and S11). Read [Before any tag that ships `exec.v2`](exec-before-any-tag.md) before anything else that changes the wire or the Go API, then the guide, [Exec: run commands in a target](exec.md), which is the contract as an author reads it.

**X1 (#87, merged 2026-11-22): the contract.** The service `omniview.sdk.exec.v2` (`proto/exec/v2/exec.proto`, generated Go beside it), the author API in `pkg/v1/exec` (`Target`, `ExecFunc`, `Proc`, `Request`, `Exit` and its constructors, `Capabilities` at two levels, `Choice` and `Option`, `Error` with `ErrorClass` and `ErrorReason`), `Describe` and `Options` served end to end (`describe.go`, `options.go`, `plugin_server_v2.go`, `register_v2.go`, `admission_v2.go`, `admission_request_v2.go`), the host's `Client` (`client.go`, `client_v2.go`), the conformance skeleton (`pkg/v1/exec/exectest`), the contract guard (`proto/exec/v2/contract_test.go` and `exec.contract.golden`), the guide and the example plugin (`examples/exec-plugin`). It was reviewed in three rounds (five shape blockers closed; the exports of `pkg/v1/exec` went from 609 to 236). It proves that `Describe` and `Options` work over a real socket with a real connection lease, that admission is strict (with fuzzers), and that the legacy lane is untouched: 12 raw HTTP/2 rows against real v0.21.0 and v0.22.0 binaries, `DIFFERENT=0` in all. `Open` and `Run` answered a typed `UNSUPPORTED` until the steps below.

**X2a (#88, merged 2026-11-23): the lifecycle primitive and the engine core.** `internal/session` (one registry and entry lifecycle over `internal/producers`: `Admit`, `End` with its cause recorded before the cancel, `Go`, `Watch`, `Removed`, `Close`), `internal/lifecycletest` (the hammer, the recorder, the goroutine baseline) and, in `pkg/v1/exec`, the engine that runs an author's `ExecFunc` with a `Proc`: `engine.go` (the supervisor), `pipes.go` (bounded queues: 256 KiB of input, 4096 runs and 64 pending drops of output; the latest-wins resizer), `classify.go` (the typed end with its source and its survival), `telemetry.go`, `start_gate.go` (`Started` at most once and before the first output), `internal/exechook` (the in-process handle) and the `exectest` fake target and stress harness. Proved in its review: 16 pair rows under `-race` (each run 60 to 300 times, at `-cpu 1` and `-cpu 2`) with one winner and a typed loser; the end-state stress for 30 s plain and under `-race` (33,646 and 1,670 commands) left 0 sessions and goroutines at baseline; a session that misbehaves never stalls another (echo max 0.48 ms against a 0.16 ms baseline); heap flat at 4.6 to 5.1 MiB over a 60 s flood with every consumer stalled; the engine into a real gRPC unix socket carries about 1.0 GB/s (the bare transport 1.17 GB/s).

**X2a-fix and X3a (#90, merged 2026-11-23): `Open` served.** The review of #88 found that abandoned functions were unbounded and that a hung `Options` or `Classify` held its slot. The fixes: an abandoned function keeps its session's place (`abandon.go`; `MaxSessions` bounds running sessions and abandoned functions together), `Options` runs under a deadline and the grace, `Classify` under a deadline, `command.Gone` frees the id, the `ErrorClass*` and `ErrorReason*` renames, and `Plugin.Shutdown`. X3a: `Open` over gRPC, one stream per session (`open_server.go`, `open_frames.go`, `open_client.go`; `Client.Open` and `Conn`): `Opened` first, then `Notice`, `Started` before the first `Output`, `Output` with offsets, `Dropped`, `Link` and one typed `Ended`; input applied in order with the stream's window as backpressure; conformance cases 1 to 5 and 18 over the wire. Proved in its review, with real go-plugin processes: a 64 MiB flood arrives byte for byte (SHA-256 equal, 769 to 806 MB/s; the legacy lane moves 10 to 35); a cancel, a severed connection, a `Close` and a kill frame mid-flood each end the session in 27 to 29 ms with the child gone and the place back; 3,002 start-and-kill attempts against `MaxSessions` 32 admitted 32 and refused 2,970 with `UNAVAILABLE`; RSS stays flat over 60 s with a stalled client; 1,000 instant sessions lost no tail.

**X2b (#91, merged 2026-11-23): `Run` and `RunStream`.** Argv only, every bound mandatory, head and tail capture with an exact account (`capture.go`, `run.go`, `run_server.go`, `run_client.go`), typed outcomes, `Client.Run`, `Client.RunStream` and `RunConn`, and the conformance bodies of rows 15 and 27 and pairs P1 and P3. The same PR closed the review of #90: the unary `Options` RPC is bounded (a deadline and the grace, at most 32 callbacks in flight per target), the controller's `Shutdown` is held until the sessions have been sent their end (an interceptor in `pkg/sdk/plugin.go`; see S1 for what is left), a wedged target is refused `ERROR_REASON_TARGET_WEDGED`, and a kill waits 50 ms for the function. It had no review of its own: the review of #92 covers it. Proved there: a function that ignores `ctx` ends `TIMED_OUT` at +51 ms (p50; 77.9 ms at most under load); a 20 GiB flood through 64 KiB caps in 1.46 s with the heap up by 1.1 MiB; the accounts are exact for blocking targets (60,796 runs, 0 off) and **wrong per stream for a lossy target that writes both streams** (T1).

**X3b and X3c (#92, merged 2026-11-24): control frames, sessions that outlive their stream, keepalive, the Kubernetes-proof fixes.** X3b: the acknowledgement of resizes (`resize.go`, the resizer in `pipes.go`), signals (`signals.go`), `close_input` with `FEATURE_STDIN_EOF`, fact queries and pushes (`facts_query.go`, `proc_control.go`, `open_control.go`). X3c: a replay ring per session (`ring.go`: 64 KiB chunks, absolute offsets, a size log, exact drop accounting, `Start.replay_bytes`), `Open{attach}` with the atomic reply and supersede (`replay.go`), `Detach` and the lease (`lease.go`: 5 minutes by default, 30 at most), a host that leaves is an implicit detach, a 60 s tombstone that holds the id (`tombstone.go`), `ListSessions`, `ReadOutput` and `EndSession` (`session_server.go`, `session_client.go`; `FEATURE_SESSION_MANAGEMENT`). The keepalive: the plugin's enforcement and server parameters (`utils.ServerKeepaliveOptions`) and the host's client parameters (`utils.HostKeepalive`, `sdk.HostKeepaliveDialOption`). The conformance Open bodies of rows 6 to 14, 16, 17, 21 to 23, 25, 26 and 28 and pairs P2 and P5, and the stress row over detach, attach, supersede, tombstones and ids. Proved in its review (all in containers limited to two CPUs): reattach is byte-exact by offset in every adversarial case (a real go-plugin process: 8,606 and 6,843 detach, cancel, sever and supersede rounds in 30 s each, SHA-256 equal; a 100-walk random soak under `-race`: 0 unannounced holes; 1,000 detach and attach cycles: heap flat at 7 to 10 MiB, 20 goroutines at every sample); every accepted control frame is delivered before the end or refused, for each of 8 kinds of end, forced both ways and under `-race`; every RPC of the lane is bounded against a hostile author; a real v0.22.1 binary works both ways with the legacy lane `DIFFERENT=0`; `buf breaking` is clean; 20 of 20 `-race` rounds pass; and the suite kills 79 of 87 mutants (the five gaps are in the gate page, [Pins and killer tests](exec-before-any-tag.md#pins-and-killer-tests)). The ten findings of the Kubernetes provider are closed by it (section 4).

## 2. What remains

Scope, contract and proofs below are the lane's step briefs, reduced to what an engineer needs. Nothing here exists in this repository at `b9cedd6` unless it says so: no `pkg/v1/exec/scan`, no `pkg/v1/exec/integration`, no `LocalProcess`, and no lane other than exec uses `internal/session`. The host and plugin steps live in their own repositories and track their status there ([omniviewdev/omniview#700](https://github.com/omniviewdev/omniview/issues/700) scopes H1a-1, H1a-2, H1a-3, H1b, K1a and K2).

### 2.1 The gate fixes come first

The wire and the Go API stay free to change only until a release carries them, so the items of [the gate](exec-before-any-tag.md) are the next work, and each X step after them lands on a lane whose shapes are final. Suggested grouping, which is not the review's: one PR for every wire edit (B1.4, B1.5, B1.7, the comments of S9 and S12, B1.10's routes and S2's epoch or S11's feature if the lead chooses them), so that the golden and the generated code change once; one for the engine fixes (T1, S1, S3, S5, S7, S8, S10); one for the session calls (S4, S6, the ownership rule); one mechanical rename (B2); one for the five pins and the two killer tests. B1.6 is the host's first consumer (H1a-1).

### 2.2 SDK steps

**X4a. Scanners: marks with trust, terminal modes, a ground-state replay start** (M; needs X3c, done; unblocks H1c, X4b and K2's decorations). *Scope:* one streaming tokenizer (ground, ESC, CSI, OSC, DCS/APC/PM strings, UTF-8; BEL and ST terminators; a sequence cut across frames; bounded state; malformed input ignored) in an importable package `pkg/v1/exec/scan`, so that the host's local provider uses the same code and never a second scanner. It feeds three opt-in consumers that never change a byte. (1) Marks (`FEATURE_MARKS`): OSC 133 `A`, `B`, `C`, `D`, OSC 633 `E` and `P;Cwd`, and OSC 7 become `Mark{kind, at_offset, exit_code, cwd, command_id, command_line, trust, duration_ms, redraw}`, tolerating a `D` without a `C`, a repeated `A` and the 133 that p10k, starship and oh-my-zsh emit on their own; two trust tiers, `NONCE` only when the session's nonce verifies (compared in constant time, never copied into a mark, a log or a span) and `UNVERIFIED` otherwise; `command_id` assigned per session; `command_line` at most 4 KiB and only with trust. (2) Terminal modes (`FEATURE_MODES`): alt screen (`?1049`, `?1047`, `?47`), bracketed paste, application cursor, focus events, cursor visibility, mouse tracking and encoding and the kitty keyboard stack, kept per ring chunk so that `Replay.start_modes` is exact and `modes` is current, and `Ended.mode_reset` and `Dropped.mode_reset` reset exactly what was set. (3) The ground-state boundary: the ring trims only to a chunk boundary at which the tokenizer is in the ground state, so that `replay.start` never lands inside an escape sequence or a UTF-8 sequence (an OSC longer than the ring is the one case where the front waits for its end); `Fact{INTEGRATION_TIER}` is pushed when the observed tier changes. *Files:* `pkg/v1/exec/scan/{scanner.go, marks.go, modes.go, nonce.go}`, the hook in the output path and a field in the ring's chunk (the only edits to X3a and X3c files), `exectest/conformance_scan.go`. *Today:* the guide's [What the SDK does not serve](exec.md#what-the-sdk-does-not-serve) lists what is absent; conformance cases 19 and 24 skip with "X4a"; the proto comments that describe the ground-state rule ([N4](exec-before-any-tag.md#notes-n1-to-n7)) become true with this step. *Proofs:* property and fuzz tests (no byte changed, no panic, bounded memory, a split at every offset gives the same marks); a forged mark that is `cat`-ed into the session is `UNVERIFIED`, with the right nonce `NONCE`, with a wrong one `UNVERIFIED`; the mode tracker against what vim, less, tmux and htop emit (recorded fixtures); a replay that starts after a dropped range begins at a ground boundary and `start_modes` equals what a fresh tracker computes over the dropped prefix; the nonce appears in no log, span, error or frame but `facts.integration.nonce`; scanner throughput of at least 1 GB/s, and the flood proof of X3a does not regress with all three consumers on (at least 300 MB/s, at most 15 ms of CPU per MB).

**X4b. Integration bootstrap for remote shells** (M; needs X4a; unblocks K2's declaration of `Capabilities.integration` and the UI's opt-in pod decorations). *Scope:* (1) clean-room scripts for bash, zsh and fish, written from the protocol and not copied (the scripts of iTerm2, Ghostty and kitty are copyleft); each emits the nonce inside its marks, drops the variable after reading it where the shell allows, and coexists with bash-preexec, p10k, starship and oh-my-zsh; (2) `Bootstrap(argv, nonce) []string` builds `[sh, -c, <script>, --, <the requested argv...>]` with the script and the nonce in argv, never typed into the user's tty, and the script then `exec`s the requested shell; (3) admission: a mode other than `OFF` needs `consent` and a target that lists it (`CONSENT_REQUIRED`, `UNSUPPORTED`), the author never sees the nonce, `Request.Argv` is the wrapped argv, and an image with no `sh` or a read-only filesystem falls back to the plain program with `NOTICE_KIND_INTEGRATION_IGNORED` and tier `NONE`; (4) one script set shared with the host's local-terminal injection: two copies are not allowed. *Files:* `pkg/v1/exec/integration/{bootstrap.go, embed.go, scripts/}`, the admission of `Start.integration` in `open_server.go`, `exectest/conformance_integration.go`. *Today:* conformance case 20 skips with "X4b". *Proofs:* a bash fixture container reaches tier `RICH` with the right `command_line` and `exit_code`, the user's own `PROMPT_COMMAND` kept and nothing printed on the tty; an `ash` fixture and a distroless fixture give tier `NONE` and no crash; no `consent` is refused; a corrupted payload leaves a working shell; the nonce canary. Nothing about it was run in a pod [INFERENCE]: spike one day on kind first (`kubectl exec -it` with the wrapper reaches the first prompt within one extra exec round trip and the first prompt carries a mark; the argv is visible in `ps` inside the container, which is accepted for a single-use nonce).

**X5. Local-process helper** (S; no dependency). `exec.LocalProcess(ctx, p, cmd)` runs an `*exec.Cmd` on a pty (unix) with size, resize, hangup then kill and a typed exit or signal, for plugins that run a local process and for a real-pty conformance rig. The child is a session leader in its own process group; a timeout or a cancel sends `SIGTERM` to the group, waits a short grace (50 to 200 ms, a parameter), then `SIGKILL` to the group; the drain of the pipes after the exit is bounded (`post_exit_drain_ms`) because a grandchild can hold them open; `IUTF8` on the master; one start of the program with a cleaned environment (no login-shell re-run). Proofs: a grandchild that holds the pipe cannot keep a run past its bound; a child that moved to a new session is still killed with the group; 200 start-and-kill cycles leave 0 processes and 0 descriptors. Windows ConPTY comes later, behind the same contract.

**X6. The registry in logs, metric and networker** (M, three PRs of S to M, one per lane). Each adopts `internal/session` (`Registry`), each runs the lifecycle hammer (`internal/lifecycletest`), and the `forbidigo` rule against `sync.WaitGroup` in `pkg/v1/**` then turns strict (`.golangci.yml` has no such rule at `b9cedd6`); `producers.Group` stays the primitive underneath.

### 2.3 Host steps (the host repository)

- **H1a-1. The provider abstraction, the legacy adapter, typed events, `GetTargetOptions`** (M; needs X1, done). The controller holds providers behind one interface (the SDK's `exec.Client` shape): `legacy` now, `local` in H1a-2, `v2` in H1b. `LegacyProvider` serves protocol-1 plugins with every capability false and the facts and the end `unknown`; a plugin's stop or crash ends each of its sessions with a typed end in the window; delivery is per session with no shared FIFO for non-legacy providers; `ListSessions` tolerates one failing plugin; every exec error goes through the public-text gate; `GetTargetOptions(plugin, connection, reference)` calls `Options`; one `Size{cols, rows}`. Typed events gain `offset` and `seq` (the host drops `seq` today) and the optional `end.source`, `end.lost`, `end.output`, `mode_reset`, `link`, `replay` and the new capabilities, all unknown for the legacy adapter. *Proofs:* `RunClientSuite` against `LegacyProvider` asserting the unknown path (it lives in `internal/exectestkit` here, which a module outside this one cannot import: decide how the host gets it when H1a-1 writes its first test, see B3); a plugin stop yields one typed end per session; a failing plugin does not fail `ListSessions`; a canary string in a plugin's stream error and stderr reaches no event, log or span; pair P6 (the legacy adapter equals the new lane on the same fake remote, for the bytes; it skips here with "H1a-1"). **B1.6 closes here** on the recommended route: the readers of the gate page are the first consumer's, proven on a host `wip/` branch if it cannot merge before the tag.
- **H1a-2. The local terminal as a provider; ordered input and credit flow moved up** (M; needs H1a-1). `LocalProvider` implements the SDK's `Client` and `Conn` over the host's terminal manager (capabilities: resize, all signals, cwd and foreground by pull, kill terminates, `host_restart` none); `CreateTerminal(target, opts, launch)` is provider-neutral; the ordered queue, `seq`, `INPUT_BACKLOG` and credit flow sit in front of every provider; the SDK's `End`, `Foreground` and `Directory` types replace the host's, with the JSON unchanged. It fills `offset` exactly, answers `Attach{from_offset}` with the atomic reply and gives `Detach` apart from `Close`. *Proofs:* the client suite against `LocalProvider`; pair P4 (it skips here with "H1a-2"); the echo of one local session while another floods drops from p50 443 ms to at most 2 ms; the events of a 64 MiB flood number at most 4,100.
- **H1a-3. Per-window release for plugin sessions** (S; needs H1a-1). Store the owner at create and end the session when its window retires, `closed_by: window`. *Proof:* 100 sessions across 20 pages with a fake provider, all 20 pages retired: 0 sessions left, one end each, the 21st page's sessions untouched; a retirement racing a create leaves none.
- **H1b. The v2 plugin provider** (M; needs X3a and X3c, done). `V2Provider` over `PluginClient.V2()`: the client keepalive dial options (decide [S11](exec-before-any-tag.md#s2-and-s11) first), a stream per session, the host's credit mapped to "stop reading `Conn.Events`", ordered input with typed backpressure, reattach and `ReadOutput` for a window reload (offsets join exactly), `Describe` cached and mapped to the UI's facts. It forwards `Ended` (lost, exit source, output completeness, last offset, mode reset), `Dropped`, `Link` and `Opened.replay` unchanged, never infers an end from a closed stream, shows `lost` as "connection lost, the command may still be running" with Restart and never as exit 0, and marks the transcript incomplete when `output` says so (`UNSPECIFIED`, which the plan calls `UNKNOWN`, or `TRUNCATED`). *Proofs:* the client suite against it with the SDK's `exectest.NewFakeTarget` over a real socket; a plugin kill ends every session within the keepalive bound as `Failed{connection}`; a reload mid-flood replays plus live to the uninterrupted transcript.
- **H1c. The local provider adopts the scanner and the atomic attach** (S to M; needs H1a-2, X3c and X4a). It imports the SDK's `exec/scan` and never keeps a second scanner, generates the per-session nonce and hands it to the shell integration it injects (the same bytes as X4b's scripts), emits typed marks, fills `modes` in the attach reply and sends `Ended.mode_reset`.
- **H-run. The bounded agent `run` tool and the UI run action** (M; needs X2b, done). A host-built `exec.Run` operation beside the logs one, with the host's own schema; `ExecPolicy` off by default and for any connection id it does not know; consent on the exact argv; clamped bounds; a two-phase audit (an argv digest and a redacted preview, env names only) before and after; typed refusals; refused when consent or audit is unavailable. The result carries the outcome (exited, signaled, timed out, cancelled, lost), the exit code and its source, both streams' completeness, the run id and `remoteMayStillRun`; a `lost` result or an unproven completeness is never shown as success, and a run that ended `lost` or whose `remote_may_survive` is not `false` is never retried. Three gate items touch it: the per-stream accounts of a lossy target (T1), `remote_may_survive` after an abandoned function (S7) and `OUTCOME_CANCELLED`, which the SDK never delivers (B1.4), so a cancelled call is the host's own status.
- **H-transport. A Wails transport spike for pty bytes** (M, optional; needs H1a-2 and X3a). A measured question (does a binary stream beat events plus base64 enough to adopt?) with a decision rule written before the run; no change to the contract.

### 2.5 F1. The flip (M; the cohort: SDK, host and plugins)

One cohort deletes the legacy lane. The SDK deletes the legacy `ExecPlugin` service (ten unary RPCs and `Stream`) and its messages, the flat `proto/exec.proto`, and the Go API of the legacy lane (`Provider`, `Handler`, `TTYHandlerFunc`, `CommandHandler`, `SessionHandler`, `SessionOptions`, `Session`, `SessionResizeInput`, `StreamInput`, `StreamOutput`, `StreamSignal`, `StreamError`, `StreamResize`, `ExecError`, `ExecSessionError` and its codes, `OutputBuffer`, `OutputSink`, `ChannelSink`, `Terminal`, `TerminalFactory`, `NewRealTerminalFactory` and the pty code, the legacy `Manager`, `ResourceSessionWireVersion` (utility wire 2 for exec), `ValidateLegacyExecWire`, the exec entries of `strictUtilityRoot`, and the fake-pipe `exectest` harness). `PlanNativeCommand` stays an independent RPC until then and moves into the new service unchanged. The host deletes `AdapterV1`, `LegacyProvider`, the legacy `CreateSession(plugin, ...)` method, `outputMux` and the widened `case 1` of `dispenseProvider`; the Kubernetes plugin has left the legacy `Handlers` in K2. *Gate:* the descriptor digest, the release table row, the conformance cases in both directions, and the floor words ("this plugin needs the new exec lane" is the existing `incompatible` cause: no new word). It rides the release that moves the protocol floor to 2, because the floor then makes every plugin a v2 plugin; until then a plugin registers the legacy service only if it supplies `Handlers` and the new one only if it supplies `Targets` (`pkg/v1/exec/plugin.go:164-166`).

## 3. Branch rules and tags

- **`feature/exec-v2` carries the lane.** Every exec v2 change, the gate fixes and every step above, goes there by PR from a worktree of `origin/feature/exec-v2`, and PRs are made **to `feature/exec-v2`**. A fix that lands on `main` reaches the branch by merging `main` into it, as #90 did with v0.22.1.
- **`main` carries the legacy lane and its repairs** (v0.22.x), and stays releasable for small fixes. It has no `exec.v2`: `git ls-tree -r origin/main --name-only | grep -c proto/exec/v2` prints `0`. Do not merge the lane, or a part of it, into `main` before the gate is cleared.
- **Never tag from `main` while `exec.v2` code would freeze** (gate item C3). A stable tag cut from a branch that contains `exec.v2` ships it for good, and the CI job `proto-breaking` baselines the latest stable tag, v0.22.1, which carries no `proto/exec/v2`, so it guards nothing of the lane until a stable tag carries the file; until then the reviewed golden is the only guard of the wire.
- **Tags only after [the gate](exec-before-any-tag.md#clearing-the-gate) is cleared.** Tags are the lead's. The host merges only against a released SDK tag, so work that needs the lane before the tag is proven on a `wip/` branch with no PR against the SDK.
- **What the plan says about pre-releases and the flip.** The plan names no pre-release. On tags it says: "X0 to X4b are additive and ride the next tag cut after they merge (T2 if they are in by then, else a later MINOR); none forces a tag of its own; F1 rides T3 with the cohort" (T2 is the plan's name for the next additive release and T3 for the cohort release that carries the flip), and that the `exec.v2` proto "is public the moment it is tagged: freeze it behind the descriptor-digest test [...] from X1 on". On the flip: only the deletion of the legacy lane needs the protocol number to move; everything in the new lane is additive and needs no bump ("the bump adds, the flip deletes"), and the deletion rides the release that moves the floor (section 2.5). The gate adds the one rule about pre-releases: C3 says that a pre-release tag (`-rc.N`) is invisible to `@latest` and to the `proto-breaking` baseline, so it freezes nothing, and the contract review's advice is that a pre-release cut earlier than the gate carries release notes saying that `proto/exec/v2` is unfrozen until the first stable tag.
- **Working rules for every PR.** Go with `GOWORK=off GOTOOLCHAIN=local`; tests first and seen red, with the fault removed to show that the test is sensitive; one commit and one push per PR (save work on a `wip/` branch with no PR until then); answer every review comment in its thread; never merge your own PR; never create a tag.

## 4. The Kubernetes spike

Repository `omniviewdev/omniview-plugin-kubernetes`, branch `wip/spike/exec-v2-provider`, head `a37f0d058b8a0febfa526cea6df71bd899c32a43` (one commit on `origin/feature/ui-next` `2b5e684`; no PR). It is the real provider that the gate asked for before any tag.

**What it proves.** A Pod provider fits the contract in 398 lines (329 of code) against the legacy 862 (648 for the Pod-only part): `Describe`, `Options`, `Exec` and `Classify` in `pkg/plugin/execv2/` (`target.go` 49 lines, `options.go` 109, `podexec.go` 188, `classify.go` 52; the spike-only `spike.go` and `ownexec.go` are not the provider). Through the real plugin binary launched as the host launches it, with the SDK's `Client.Open`, on kind v1.34.0 (containerd 2.1.3): TTY and non-TTY, stdout and stderr apart, the size at open (`stty size` 30 100 at the first command, 40 120 after a resize), exit codes, typed refusals, `Close` and a cancelled stream, `Lost` for every break of the API-server leg, and a 64 MiB flood at 170 to 185 MB/s (kubectl 182 to 191); the picker's states predicted whether an exec would work for 10 of 10 containers. It was built against the SDK at `abbcad0` (#90), so the fixes of #91 and #92 are not in it.

**The rig.** `hack/kproof/` in the same branch: a frame-aware tap proxy between the plugin and the API server records what the API server really sends, and the scenarios run through `Client.Open`. Direct probes (`pkg/plugin/execv2/kind_test.go`, skipped unless `KPROOF_KUBECONFIG` is set) call the provider's `ExecFunc` against the cluster with no SDK in between. 228 sessions went through the tap.

**The ten contract findings and where each landed in the SDK** (all ten run and closed in the review of #92):

| # | Finding of the spike | Where it landed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | client-go's `nil` is a Success and an empty channel alike, and after a kubelet or containerd restart the API server sends a Success for a command that did not exit 0 | `Exited{0, TRANSPORT}` is the arm for it: `exec.ExitOfStreamError` and `exectest.CodeExitError` (`exit_stream.go`, `exectest/stream_errors.go`), the guide's [Ending a command over a client library](exec.md#ending-a-command-over-a-client-library), the proto comments of `Exited` and `EXIT_SOURCE_TRANSPORT`; what is left is B1.3 and S12 |
| 2 | `Started` has no honest trigger for a quiet command | `exec.OnFirstRead` (`exit_stream.go`) and the SDK's start gate (`start_gate.go`): `Started` is always before the first `Output`, made from `Opened` when output comes first |
| 3 | the default choice is not in `Request.Selection` | the function sees the resolved choice of every option (`options_resolve.go`) |
| 4 | `Options` runs at every `Open` | an options cache used only by the admission of an `Open` or a `Run` (`options_cache.go`: 5 s, 64 entries, keyed by the whole identity of the request) |
| 5 | typed classification fails for the upgrade's own refusals (403 and 401 arrive as text) | `exec.ErrorFromHTTPStatus` (`error_http.go`) and a default classification that reads `HTTPStatus() int` or `StatusCode() int`; the author's `Classify` reads the text with it (`examples/exec-plugin/classify_snippet_test.go`) |
| 6 | `close_input` is not served | `ClientFrame.close_input` with `FEATURE_STDIN_EOF` (X3b) |
| 7 | an attach's `Opened.facts.program` is wrong (it runs no program) | `Choice.NoProgram`, wire `Choice.no_program = 8` (`choice_capabilities.go`) |
| 8 | `replay_bytes` is refused `UNSUPPORTED` | served: `Start.replay_bytes`, a ring of 1 KiB to 4 MiB (`ring.go`, `replay.go`) |
| 9 | `Stall=LOSSY` drops bytes for a consumer slower than about 100 MB/s | the drops are counted exactly (`Dropped`, `Ended.dropped_bytes`, `TRUNCATED`); the guide says to declare `StallLossy` for Kubernetes ([Which stall behaviour to declare](exec.md#which-stall-behaviour-to-declare)) |
| 10 | a Kubernetes close leaves the process running (18 of 18 survived) | `remote_may_survive` present and true for `KillClosesStreamOnly` on every end the SDK makes (`classify.go`) |

Not served by the SDK and needed by the same provider: `Classify` has no request (the facts of a refusal come from `ExecFunc`), `LinkHealth` cannot be implemented over client-go (the executor pings every 5 s and gives up after 61 s with no hook), the wait for an ephemeral container that is starting has no place in the contract, the vocabularies of `Choice.Kind` and `Choice.Detail` are the provider's to invent, and the incarnation check leaves a race between the read of the Pod and the exec.

## 6. How to run the conformance suite against a target

```go
func TestConformance(t *testing.T) { exectest.Conformance(t, myTarget()) }

// a target with an Options callback: pass the id of the resource it knows
func TestConformanceOptions(t *testing.T) {
	exectest.Conformance(t, myTarget(), exectest.ConformanceResource("web-0"))
}
```

`exectest.Conformance` serves the target through the real SDK over a unix socket and runs, as subtests, the registration checks, the client suite, the numbered cases and pair tests of the `engine` group, and the `options wiring` checks when the target has an `Options` callback (`pkg/v1/exec/exectest/conformance.go:93`). The cases and the five rows that skip are listed in [Test your target](exec.md#test-your-target). Run it with the CI form:

```sh
GOWORK=off GOTOOLCHAIN=local go test ./pkg/v1/exec/... -count=1                 # the Exec contract job
GOWORK=off GOTOOLCHAIN=local go test -race ./... -count=1                          # the SDK suite (race) job
OMNIVIEW_COMPAT_V0210=1 GOWORK=off GOTOOLCHAIN=local go test ./pkg/v1/exec/ -run '^TestCompatV0210$' -count=1 -timeout 90m -v   # legacy compatibility
```

Run anything heavy, a flood, a race loop or a mutant, in a container limited to two CPUs and never on a host: [How to verify the gate](exec-before-any-tag.md#how-to-verify-the-gate) has the command, the race loop and the way to run a mutant against a test. To test one function of your own, build a rig with `exectest.NewProcRig` (the same section of the guide); `exectest.NewFakeTarget` is a scriptable target.
